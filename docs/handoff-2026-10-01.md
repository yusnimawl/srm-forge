## What v0a does
Requirements → Latin-hypercube candidates over 6/4, 8/6, 12/8, 24/16 → stage 1 (closed-form torque upper bound + mass limit) → stage 2 (reduced `srmcore` map + minimal drive model: single-pulse voltage control, hysteresis current limit, ideal commutation, phase A only, no mutual coupling, copper loss only) → scores and a Pareto set over (mass, power density, copper-only efficiency at base speed). Peak-only, **unvalidated**.

## Findings that shape what comes next
1. **0 of 6 evaluated designs feasible** for 10 kW / 32 N·m / 3000–9000 rpm / 400 V (torque ratios 0.02–1.82, power ratios 0.01–0.56). Diagnosis (analysis, not measured): turns per pole are sampled independently of bus voltage and max speed. At fixed current density, ψ ∝ N, i ∝ 1/N, R ∝ N², so changing turns is an exact speed rescale of the same FEM map. The torque-ratio-1.82 design is probably fine geometry with wrong turns.
2. **Cost ~200 s per design** (spec assumed 20 s). Per-point FEM solves dominate (~2.5 s + 1.6 s per point). The owner says time does not matter, so do not chase speed.
3. **Drive-model angle sensitivity:** the envelope torque moves 10–35 % between 10 and 12 angles. The 12×8 reference shares its angle set with 12×5, so the grid study cannot validate angle discretisation. No check against a 24/30-angle reference exists. The drive model also forbids flux past alignment (conservative at high speed, size unmeasured) and v0 has no inverter current limit.
4. Stage-1 torque bound is optimistic; its false-drop rate against stage 2 is unmeasured.
5. Open engine items from M4 (held-out torque 3 % provisional, La screening error 2.6–3.0 %, no mirror-symmetry check, no test asserting held-out accuracy on a realistic grid, stale-cache risk because only the srmcore version is in the cache key) are in spec §10 and `docs/m4-handoff.md`.

1. **Choose turns after the map.** One map per geometry at a reference turn count; rescale (ψ·k, i/k, R·k²) and scan N on a log grid in the drive model; pick N meeting torque/power, or maximise the worst of the two ratios. Turns leave the generator's sampled dimensions; stage 1's bound does not depend on turns. Exact, not a speed trick.
