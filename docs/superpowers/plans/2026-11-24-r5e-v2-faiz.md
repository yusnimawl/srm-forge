# R5e gate V2 (Faiz 2009) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Run gate V2: our 2-D thermal solver (`srmcore.thermal`) on the Faiz 2009 8/6 stator with the paper's
inputs, compared with their 2-D result (Fig. 10, Fig. 9, Table VI), with the pass rule of the spec, and record the
verdict, the input difference found and the remaining gap.

**Architecture:** test-side helper `tests/faiz_8_6.py` builds the Faiz stator from `RadialSRMParams` (EM mesh ->
`thermal_domain` -> stator only), assigns their materials, losses and boundaries, and makes a `ThermalProblem`; the
inside air of Fig. 8 is followed by rebuilding the solver every time step with the inside-air lump at that time's
temperature (public API only). `examples/faiz_thermal_check.py` runs the cases, meshes and time steps and writes
`docs/validation/r5_v2_faiz.json`. srmcore does not change.

**Tech Stack:** Python 3.12+, numpy, scipy, PyMuPDF (digitiser only).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-05-r5-thermal-stress-design.md` §8, gate V2; inputs in
`docs/r5-sources.md` §9.1 (V0, PR #41).

Geometry read off Fig. 11(a) (17.05 px/mm from the 125 mm OD; pole width 11.56 mm against 11.61 from 21 deg, a
check): liner 0.997 mm on the pole flank (17 px on every row), 0.95 to 1.10 mm at the slot top; wedge 2.205 mm radial
beside both flanks (a straight chord in their drawing, an arc at r_in in ours); slot top at r 49.6 to 50.0 mm; pole
parallel-sided (taper 0); slot-top fillets about 1.3 mm in their drawing, 0 in ours (ours would lower the coil by two
fillet radii). Frame: not drawn in Fig. 11; Fig. 12(b) to (e) read 52.0 degC at the Table I values and Fig. 12(f)
reads 52.0 only at 5 mm (51.3 at 10 mm), so the nominal frame is most likely 5 mm, an inference.

1. The as-printed frame fails at every thickness in their 5 to 25 mm range: 3.8 to 8.5 K high at 119 min, 2.9 to
   5.5 K high in the constant-air steady state.
2. With the frame lump of their own network (Table V: R1 0.57 K/W, C1 5298 J/K; the R1-C1 node of Fig. 3 is the
   only one with a path to ambient and a large capacity, so it is read as the frame, an inference) the late state
   agrees: 52.5 against 52.1 (Fig. 10) and 52.6 (Table VI C) at 119 min, 41.5 against 40.7 at constant air. Where
   the agreement starts is for the full runs to show.
3. Early rise: theirs is 5.4 K in the first minute, ours 2.0 K (adiabatic copper with Table II and Table IV gives
   2.26 K/min), a factor about 2.4. Hypothesis, not fact: neither the winding capacity nor the loss density alone
   explains it. Copper loss x 2.4 matches minute 1 but is 23 K high at 119 min (ruled out). Winding rho c x 0.25
   closes 1 to 5 min only; 10 to 30 min stay 2 to 4 K low and the late state does not move. The R1 conductance with
   the printed frame capacity fits 30 min within 0.6 K but is 3.3 K high at 119 min. Their 2-D model most likely
   differs from the printed inputs in more than one place (frame boundary, and something that heats the winding
   faster early); the paper does not let us identify which.
4. The time step has no effect: 5 s and 30 s steps give the same curve to 0.1 K.
5. Energy balance checks: steady losses 40.9 W leave as 20.8 W to ambient and 20.1 W to the inside air; part areas
   match Table I (yoke 4420 mm2 against 4418; slot 2976 against 2980).
